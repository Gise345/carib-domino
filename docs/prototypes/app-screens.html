Screen Kit

Every tab and mode screen on one chrome: a carved header with a brass back ring, lacquered cards on the wood, and the nav along the bottom. The accent is the brass node from the tile's centre divider — the same detail that sits on every domino, reused as the app's one piece of metal so the interface belongs to the board rather than floating over it.

The back control

One control, used everywhere a screen can be left. A brass ring with a chevron — round like the divider node, and the only circular thing in the chrome, so it never competes with a card or a button for attention.

Dismiss

Why a ring and not an arrow on its own. A bare chevron on artwork disappears — it has no edge of its own and the wood behind it keeps changing. The ring gives it a constant silhouette wherever it lands, and makes the tap target obvious at the size a thumb actually needs.

The tabs

Each one currently renders as text on a transparent panel. These give them a subject: what the screen is for decides its shape, rather than every tab being the same stack of rows.

Settings

Volumes are sliders, not On/Off — the reason to open this screen is usually "quieter", not "off".

The vibration row says what it cannot do. Turn nudges ignore this toggle by design, so the screen admits it in a line rather than letting a player switch it off and wonder why their phone still buzzes.

The mode screens

All three ask the same three questions — who is at the table, what the format is, what it costs — so they share one shape. What differs is the seat map, because that is the actual difference between the modes.

The code is the screen. Everything else is secondary to getting it to the people you want at the table.

Screens are drawn at the real 800 × 1730 canvas ratio. Type is Baloo 2 and Nunito, the pair DESIGN_SYSTEM.md §3 specifies. Colours are §2 tokens throughout; the wood is board.png from the project. Numbers shown are the real ones — 1,000 entry and the 2,000 key bonus come from ADR 0016, the 30-second clock from the turn timer.
