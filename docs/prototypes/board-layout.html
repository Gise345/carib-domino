Four fixes in one sweep: flat, thick, white tiles at a much larger size, the top and bottom hands dropped away from their edges, the floating profiles moved off the tiles they were covering, and the collapsed scoreboard tightened. Everything is drawn in real canvas units, so the numbers below port straight across.

The board, before and after

Both boards are the live 800 × 1730 canvas at 40%. The hatched red blocks are measured overlaps, not sketches — the top profile covers its hand by its full 140 × 180, and each side profile sits entirely inside its hand column.

The tile

The current tile is a small cream rectangle with a hard 3, -3 drop shadow faking depth. The new one is flat: a true white body, a hard dark edge instead of a shadow, a heavier centre rule and pips grown from 18% to 22% of the tile's short side. Shown at true relative scale.

Why the shadow goes. A drop shadow offset down-right implies a light source, and the board already has one — the string lights overhead. Two conflicting light directions is what was making the tiles read as stickers on the felt. A flat body with a hard edge reads as a solid object at any angle, and it holds up far better at the new size.

Collapsed scoreboard

Collapsed, the panel should be a title bar and nothing else. Right now the body's padding is still being counted, so roughly 46 units of dead space sit above and below the word.

The one real conflict

Your hand and the turn clock want the same corner. Seven tiles at the new 84-unit width need 624 units. The clock occupies x 24–132 in that bottom strip, which leaves exactly 626 to its right — so the hand fits, but only by docking at x 150 instead of centring. That is what the board above shows. The alternative is centring the hand and shrinking the clock into the action bar beside Last Play, which frees the full width but undoes the placement you just signed off. I went with keeping your clock. Say the word if you'd rather have the hand centred.
